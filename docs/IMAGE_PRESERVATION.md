# Measuring what the image edit preserved

> Measured on 2026-08-28 against a live East US Foundry resource, using
> `MAI-Image-2.5-Flash` for generation and `MAI-Image-2.5` for the edit.

"Controlled editing" is easy to claim and easy to eyeball. This document records the
measurement instead, so the claim in the README and in the write-up is checkable.

## Reproduce it

```bash
python scripts/measure_preservation.py
```

The script needs Pillow and NumPy. Pillow is in `requirements.txt`; NumPy installs with
Streamlit. It reads the two committed frames in `assets/images/` and prints the table below.

## The two frames

| File | How it was produced |
|---|---|
| `assets/images/hydrate_photo_before.png` | Generated by `MAI-Image-2.5-Flash`, 12.7s, prompt below |
| `assets/images/hydrate_photo_after.png` | The `before` frame edited by `MAI-Image-2.5`, 28.4s, using `DEFAULT_PROMPT` from `demos/image_edit.py` |

Both are 1024x1024. The product and the brand are fictional.

### Generation prompt

```
Photorealistic commercial product photograph, shot on a 50mm lens at f/2.8. A tall matte
teal sports water bottle stands on a light oak table. The bottle carries a clean white
paper label with the brand name 'CONTOSO' in small dark letters above the word 'HYDRATE'
in large bold teal letters, and below that a narrow amber banner with the words 'Summer
Edition' in dark serif type. A white ceramic coffee cup on a saucer sits on the table to
the right of the bottle, slightly behind it. On the left, a woman in a plain grey knit
sweater is visible from the shoulders down to the waist, her hand resting on the table,
her face out of frame above the top edge. The background is a plain warm grey studio wall,
softly out of focus. Soft directional window light from the left, gentle specular highlight
down the left edge of the bottle, soft shadows falling to the right on the table. Muted
natural colour palette, high detail, sharp focus on the label, shallow depth of field.
```

All three text elements on the label rendered legibly, which is what makes the rename
demo work at all.

### Edit prompt

`DEFAULT_PROMPT` in `demos/image_edit.py`, also reproduced in `docs/PROMPTS.md`. It asks for
three changes and preservation of everything else.

## Method

Two numbers per region:

- **pixels**: the share of pixels whose summed RGB delta exceeds 24.
- **structure**: the Pearson correlation of gradient magnitude between the two frames. It
  survives a brightness or color shift and collapses when the geometry underneath changes,
  which is what separates "the model repainted this" from "the model relit this".

Structure needs texture to correlate. Regions whose mean gradient magnitude falls below 6
are reported as flat and left for visual inspection rather than scored.

```
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[change] Label text: Summer to Miami        60.2%       0.49   edit applied
[change] Coffee cup and saucer              94.2%       0.09   edit applied
[change] Background wall                    98.1%       0.01   edit applied
[keep  ] Bottle body below the label         1.1%       0.57   preserved
[keep  ] Bottle cap                         16.6%       0.96   preserved
[keep  ] HYDRATE wordmark                   40.1%       0.95   relit, geometry intact
[keep  ] Person, sweater and shoulder        0.8%       0.84   preserved
[keep  ] Person, hand on the table           0.9%       0.95   preserved
[keep  ] Table, left of the bottle          70.9%       0.51   flat region (energy 3.1), check by eye
```

## Reading the results

All three requested edits landed. The product body, the cap, the person and her hand came
back essentially untouched.

Two regions deserve the detail:

- **The table** registers 71% of pixels changed, which reads like a failure. It is not. Side
  by side it is the same oak with the same grain, slightly warmer, because the model relit
  the foreground to match a sunset that was not in the original frame. The region is too
  smooth for the structure number to mean anything, which is why the script says so instead
  of scoring it.
- **The `HYDRATE` wordmark** registers 40% of pixels changed at a structure correlation of
  0.95. The label was re-rendered and the letterforms survived it.

The takeaway for anyone measuring an editing model: preservation is not pixel identity. A
naive pixel diff would have reported a wrecked table that had in fact been relit correctly.
Decide what you are measuring before you claim control.

## Caveats

One generation and one edit, on one prompt, against one deployment, on one day. The region
boxes were placed by hand for this frame and do not transfer to a different image. This is a
recorded observation, not a benchmark.
